# Mission Design: "Out There" Style
## Design Philosophy

**Core Inspiration: Out There over FTL**
- **Exploration > Combat**: Narrative encounters, mystery, discovery
- **Resource Scarcity**: Every choice has weight - fuel, power, supplies
- **Atmosphere**: Loneliness, wonder, cosmic horror, first contact
- **Meaningful Choices**: No perfect solutions, only trade-offs
- **Consequences**: Persistent effects across the journey

---

## Mission Types

### 1. **Signal Encounters** (Discovery-Focused)
Unknown signals requiring investigation with resource investment.

**Structure:**
- Detect anomalous signal (costs power to analyze)
- Choose approach vector (fuel cost varies)
- Initial scan reveals partial information
- Decision point: investigate fully or move on
- Outcome depends on resources invested + choices

**Example Scenarios:**
- **Derelict Beacon**: Ancient ship broadcasting distress. Salvage tech/fuel vs risk hull damage
- **Quantum Signature**: Scientific phenomenon. Spend power to study (gain knowledge/data) or ignore
- **Bio-Signs**: Life detected. First contact protocol - peaceful, cautious, or retreat
- **Encrypted Transmission**: Alien code. Decode with power/time or continue journey

**Resource Tensions:**
- Investigation costs fuel (deviation from course)
- Analysis costs power (scanning systems)
- Time pressure (life support, mission deadlines)
- Risk vs reward (potential damage vs salvage)

---

### 2. **Resource Crisis Missions** (Survival-Focused)
Critical shortage forces difficult decisions.

**Structure:**
- Resource depleting rapidly (fuel leak, power drain, O2 failure)
- Multiple partial solutions available
- Each solution requires sacrificing something else
- Time pressure creates urgency
- Outcome affects future capability

**Example Scenarios:**

**"Dead Zone" - Fuel Critical:**
- Stranded far from station, fuel nearly empty
- Options:
  - Vent cargo for lighter mass (lose trade goods/equipment)
  - Emergency power-to-fuel converter (drain batteries, disable systems)
  - Distress call (attracts help OR pirates)
  - Dangerous gravity slingshot (risky maneuver, potential damage)

**"Dark Ship" - Power Failure:**
- Main power offline, batteries draining
- Options:
  - Emergency reactor restart (risk explosion)
  - Shut down life support temporarily (health damage)
  - Drain fuel cells (lose mobility)
  - Call for rescue tow (expensive, reputation hit)

**"Breathless" - Oxygen Depletion:**
- Life support compromised
- Options:
  - Reroute power from engines (stranded)
  - Use emergency oxygen (single-use, limited)
  - Induce medical coma (risky, lose time)
  - Harvest oxygen from nearby comet (dangerous EVA)

---

### 3. **Faction Crossroads** (Diplomatic-Focused)
Choices that affect factional relationships and future opportunities.

**Structure:**
- Encounter involving competing faction interests
- Choice affects reputation with multiple factions
- No option satisfies everyone
- Long-term consequences (access to stations, missions, tech)

**Example Scenarios:**

**"Contested Salvage":**
- Valuable derelict claimed by two factions
- Corporate claims legal ownership (offer payment)
- Independent miners claim salvage rights (offer tech mod)
- Take without permission (hostile reputation, free salvage)
- Destroy it (both sides hate you, but neutral outcome)

**"Refugee Cargo":**
- Refugees fleeing persecution request passage
- Government offers bounty for their return
- Smugglers offer to take them (suspicious)
- Keep them aboard (strain life support, possible reward later)
- Deny them (clean hands, but moral weight)

**"Border Incident":**
- Caught in military engagement zone
- Side A asks for sensor data (military intelligence)
- Side B demands neutrality (economic benefits)
- Sell data to both (short-term profit, long-term distrust)
- Flee the region (safe but lose opportunity)

---

### 4. **Ancient Mystery** (Exploration-Focused)
Alien ruins, precursor technology, cosmic phenomena.

**Structure:**
- Discover something unusual and ancient
- Investigation requires commitment (time, resources)
- Partial information creates mystery
- Choices lead to different discoveries
- Some mysteries remain unsolved

**Example Scenarios:**

**"Monolith":**
- Massive alien structure emitting patterns
- Options:
  - Full scan protocol (days of study, high power cost) → Deep knowledge
  - Quick survey (hours, moderate cost) → Partial understanding
  - Take artifact sample (quick, risky) → Unknown tech
  - Document and leave (minimal cost) → Sell data

**"Tomb Ship":**
- Derelict of unknown origin, impossible age
- Options:
  - Board and explore (danger: traps, degradation, unknown)
  - Scan from distance (safe but limited data)
  - Tow to station (massive fuel cost, huge payout OR disaster)
  - Call scientists (share discovery, lose control)

**"Living Nebula":**
- Cloud formation shows signs of intelligence
- Options:
  - Attempt communication (spend power/time)
  - Collect samples (may anger it)
  - Navigate through carefully (fuel efficient but slow)
  - Avoid entirely (safe but miss discovery)

---

### 5. **Moral Dilemma** (Ethics-Focused)
No good choices, only what you can live with.

**Structure:**
- Situation with multiple bad outcomes
- Player values tested (pragmatism vs ethics)
- No objectively right answer
- Emotional weight in narrative
- Consequences reflect choice

**Example Scenarios:**

**"Life Pod Calculus":**
- Multiple distress calls, only fuel to reach one
- Pod A: 20 civilians, commercial ship
- Pod B: 3 scientists, critical research data
- Pod C: 5 children, school transport
- Pod D: 1 admiral, military VIP with reward
- Or ignore all and preserve fuel for mission

**"Plague Ship":**
- Medical vessel requests docking, has cure for outbreak
- But crew is infected with different disease
- Accept them (risk infection, save thousands)
- Refuse (stay safe, pandemic spreads)
- Offer supplies only (middle ground, may not be enough)
- Destroy ship (prevent spread, mass murder)

**"Colony's End":**
- Failing colony, final evacuation
- Your ship: 50 passenger capacity
- Population: 200 people
- Criteria for selection: young, skilled, paying, lottery, none?
- Leave some behind (who decides who lives?)
- Make multiple trips (probably not enough fuel)
- Call for help (may arrive too late)

## Mission Chain Architecture

### Persistent Universe State
Missions create lasting changes:
- **Reputation**: Faction standing affects available missions
- **Knowledge**: Discovered information unlocks new options
- **Resources**: Scarcity creates cascading challenges
- **Tech**: Found/traded equipment changes capabilities
- **Crew**: Morale/health affected by decisions

### Branching Narrative Structure
```
Main Journey Arc
├── Signal Encounters (procedural discovery)
├── Resource Crises (scarcity-driven)
├── Faction Crossroads (reputation gates)
├── Ancient Mysteries (exploration rewards)
└── Moral Dilemmas (player identity)
```

### Progressive Revelation
Early game: Simple choices, clear outcomes
Mid game: Complex trade-offs, uncertain consequences
Late game: Chains of prior decisions matter, callbacks, themes

## Narrative Themes (Out There Style)

### 1. **Isolation & Wonder**
- Vast empty spaces between encounters
- Each discovery feels significant
- Radio silence creates tension
- Occasional transmissions break loneliness

### 2. **Scarcity & Trade-offs**
- Never enough resources for everything
- Every choice has opportunity cost
- Success means managing losses
- Failure is running out (fuel, power, hope)

### 3. **First Contact Uncertainty**
- Alien encounters are mysterious
- Communication is difficult/impossible
- Intentions unclear
- Risk vs curiosity tension

### 4. **Cosmic Horror Elements**
- Things beyond comprehension
- Ancient ruins with unknown purpose
- Phenomena that break known physics
- Some questions have no answers

### 5. **Ethical Gray Zones**
- Survival vs morality
- Individual vs collective good
- Progress vs preservation
- Knowledge vs safety

## Integration with Existing Systems

### Leverage Current Mechanics:

**Resource Management:**
- Fuel scarcity drives route choices
- Power limitations force system prioritization
- Life support creates time pressure
- Hull integrity affects risk tolerance

**Dynamic Event System:**
- Expand event types with narrative depth
- Add choice trees to encounters
- Implement consequence tracking
- Create event chains with callbacks

**Faction System:**
- Gate missions behind reputation
- Create conflicting interests
- Build faction-specific narrative arcs
- Reputation affects dialogue/options

**Economy:**
- Resource scarcity creates trade dilemmas
- Economic desperation drives choices
- Markets reflect player actions
- Trade becomes narrative vehicle

**Station Interactions:**
- Mission boards offer story missions
- NPCs remember player choices
- Station state reflects universe events
- Safe havens between dangers

## Technical Implementation Plan

### Phase 1: Core Narrative Engine
```typescript
interface NarrativeMission {
  id: string;
  type: MissionType; // Signal, Crisis, Faction, Mystery, Moral
  title: string;
  description: string;

  // Trigger conditions
  triggerConditions: {
    location?: LocationType;
    reputation?: ReputationRequirement;
    resources?: ResourceState;
    knowledge?: KnowledgeUnlocked[];
  };

  // Story structure
  stages: MissionStage[];
  choices: Choice[];
  consequences: Consequence[];

  // Integration
  resourceCosts: ResourceCost;
  reputationEffects: ReputationChange;
  knowledgeGain: Knowledge[];

  // Atmosphere
  mood: 'wonder' | 'dread' | 'mystery' | 'desperation' | 'awe';
  narrativeWeight: 'minor' | 'major' | 'critical';
}
```

### Phase 2: Choice & Consequence System
```typescript
interface Choice {
  id: string;
  text: string;
  description: string;

  // Requirements
  requires?: {
    fuel?: number;
    power?: number;
    time?: number;
    tech?: string[];
    reputation?: ReputationLevel;
  };

  // Outcomes
  outcomes: Outcome[];
  probabilistic?: boolean; // Some choices have uncertain outcomes
  irreversible?: boolean;  // Can't undo this

  // Narrative
  flavorText: string;
  consequences: string[]; // What happens next
}
```

### Phase 3: Knowledge & Discovery System
```typescript
interface Knowledge {
  id: string;
  category: 'alien' | 'precursor' | 'scientific' | 'faction' | 'navigation';
  title: string;
  description: string;

  // Effects
  unlocks?: string[]; // New missions/options
  techBonus?: TechUpgrade;
  marketValue?: number;

  // Narrative
  loreText: string;
  fragments?: KnowledgeFragment[]; // Piece together over time
}
```

### Phase 4: Atmospheric Systems
```typescript
interface AtmosphericEvent {
  // Environmental storytelling
  ambientMessages: string[];      // Background chatter, signals
  visualEffects: VisualEffect[];  // Nebulae, phenomena
  soundscape?: SoundState;        // Radio static, hums, silence

  // Pacing
  tension: number;     // 0-100, affects event frequency
  isolation: number;   // Time since last encounter
  mystery: number;     // Unexplained phenomena nearby
}
```

## Content Generation Strategy

### Procedural Narrative
- **Templates**: 50+ base mission templates
- **Variables**: Faction, location, resources, player state
- **Combination**: Mix elements for variety
- **Seeded**: Consistent within universe seed

### Hand-Crafted Moments
- **Key Encounters**: 10-15 signature missions
- **Narrative Anchors**: Memorable story beats
- **Mystery Arc**: Overarching questions
- **Climactic Choices**: High-stakes decisions

### Balancing Act
- 70% procedural variety
- 30% crafted experiences
- Procedural missions feel hand-made
- Crafted missions feel organic

---

## Success Metrics

**"Out There" Feel Achieved When:**
- Players feel tension over fuel gauge
- Choices provoke "I wonder what if..."
- Discoveries feel rewarding and mysterious
- Moral dilemmas create discussion
- Each run tells a unique story
- Survival feels earned, not guaranteed
- The universe feels vast and strange

**Avoid:**
- Combat as default solution
- Perfect runs with no sacrifice
- Obvious optimal choices
- Predictable outcomes
- Disposable encounters
- Resource abundance
- Hand-holding narratives
